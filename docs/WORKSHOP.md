# Redemptive Build Workshop: Recuperative Care Intake

Oct 12, 2026 · Mark Warrick · Snapshot of the live doc: <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9869fe0d-46a0-4cfe-a39b-a87d6e5aa029>

A guided prep that walks a team through the FaithTech Playbook 2.0's Redemptive Create Cycle (Lament, Discern, Build, Steward) to arrive at a tool for hospital referrals into Illumination Health + Home's recuperative care. It starts from the people and the pain, not from the app we already built. The existing app stays sealed until the last session, when the team decides whether to keep it, change it or start over.

## The one-hour plan

One meeting, all six steps, with the clock kept by someone who isn't building. Don't talk about the existing app until 0:50.

| Time | Step | What we do | We leave with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:05 | Open | Short prayer; read Matthew 25:35–36; state the ground rule | |
| 0:05–0:12 | 0. Foundations | Each person names which of the three enemies (magic machine, limitless self, killer app) pulls hardest on this project. Agree who must always decide about a patient and how patient data is treated | A 2–3 sentence covenant |
| 0:12–0:27 | 1. Lament | The staff in the room (and anyone who did a listening visit beforehand) tell one real, de-identified referral story each. Write down who hurts, when and what it costs them | A problem statement with no technology in it |
| 0:27–0:40 | 2. Discern | One option each for reject, receive, reimagine and create. Choose one | The chosen response, 3 guardrails, 2 stop conditions |
| 0:40–0:47 | 3. Build | Rank the pains; the top one becomes the first slice. Name who tries each slice | The first 3 slices, in order |
| 0:47–0:50 | 4. Steward | Who looks after it, and the top items that must be true before real patient data goes in | An owner and a release gate |
| 0:50–1:00 | 5. Open the envelope | Compare the existing app with what we just wrote down | Keep, change or start over, and the next step |

**Before the hour (optional, but it's what makes Lament real):** ask one coordinator and one nurse, and if possible a former client, the Lament listening questions and bring their exact words.

The sections below are the full version of each step, for the facilitator to draw on or for follow-up sessions.

## How to run it

The full version runs as six sessions of about two hours each; the one-hour plan above compresses it into one meeting. Every session ends with the playbook's "before moving forward" questions; the team doesn't move on until it can answer them honestly.

**Who is in the room.** The playbook asks us to draw near to the people most affected, so the builders are the minority.

| Seat | Who | Why they are needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builders | 2–3 developers or designers | Will make whatever gets made |
| Referral coordinator | 1–2 Illumination intake staff | Receives every referral and owns the two-hour decision |
| Referrer | A hospital social worker, case manager or discharge nurse | Fills in the referral, often at the end of a shift |
| Admitting nurse (LVN) | Illumination medical coordinator | Receives the patient and lives with what intake missed |
| Care Coordinator or Housing Case Manager | Illumination housing staff | Carries the person from the bed toward housing |
| Lived experience | Someone who has been through recuperative care, invited through Illumination with consent and paid for their time | The person the whole system is supposed to serve |
| Prayer and Scripture lead | A pastor or mature believer on the team | Keeps the spiritual practices from being skipped |
| Facilitator | One person who does not build | Holds time, keeps the envelope sealed, records outputs |

**Ground rules.**

- **The envelope stays sealed.** The existing app, its requirements doc and its screenshots stay closed until Session 5. Anyone who has seen it speaks from what people said, not from what was built. If someone catches themselves describing a feature, they rephrase it as the need behind it.
- **No solutions in Sessions 0 and 1.** Ideas that come up go on a "parking lot" sheet and wait for Discern.
- **Fake data only.** Any form, fax or record used in exercises is invented or fully de-identified.
- **Open with prayer, close with the exit questions.** Each session starts with a short reading from that chapter and ends with the questions as a written check-out.

**Playbook pages per session.** Page numbers are the printed ones in the Playbook 2.0.

| Session | Playbook part | Pages | Output the session must produce |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Foundations | A Different Way; The Way of Technology; A Way of Being Redemptive | 16–62 | A team covenant on power, person and purpose |
| 1. Lament | Lament | 70–74 | A pain map and a problem statement written in human terms |
| 2. Discern | Discern | 75–79 | A named response (Reject, Receive, Reimagine or Create), guardrails and stop conditions |
| 3. Build | Build | 80–85 | A sliced build plan with pause points and a trade-off log |
| 4. Steward | Steward | 86–90 | A stewardship plan and a release gate |
| 5. Open the envelope | The cycle returns to Lament | 91–94 | Keep, change or start over, with reasons |

Open question: whether Illumination can invite a former client to take part.

## Session 0: Foundations

Before looking at the problem, the team agrees on how it will see technology and what posture it will build from. The output is a one-page team covenant everyone signs.

**Opening reading.** Matthew 25:35–36 (a stranger welcomed, the sick looked after) and Genesis 1:26–27 (every person bears God's image).

**Exercise 1: the four Gospel lenses (30 min).** Apply the lenses to *how a sick person without a home leaves a hospital today*, not to any software.

| Lens | Playbook question | Ask about discharge and referral |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creation | What good purpose did God intend? | What does good care for a sick neighbor with nowhere to go look like? What gifts do hospital and Illumination staff already bring? |
| Deception | How has sin distorted us and our technology? | Where are people treated as beds to clear, cases to close or paperwork to move? Where do fax machines, portals and checklists hide a person? |
| Redemption | How does Christ redeem and redirect our making? | What would it look like for this hand-off to tell the truth and honor the patient, the referrer and the coordinator? |
| Restoration | What does this anticipate about God's renewal? | What small signs of the coming city could a better hand-off carry: welcome, rest, a next step toward home? |

**Exercise 2: the three enemies (25 min).** Each person writes where they feel each pull in *this* project, then shares one.

- **Magic Machine Myth.** Trusting an AI's reading of a handwritten medical form as if it were the truth. Treating "the system approved it" as wisdom.
- **Limitless Self.** Builders who can now make a working app in a weekend. The pull to build everything, for every role, because we can.
- **Killer App Trap.** Believing software can fix homelessness, an overloaded hospital or a two-hour decision promise on its own.

**Exercise 3: three postures grid (40 min).** On a wall, draw the playbook's grid. For a referral intake tool, write what each posture would actually do. Fill the Reckless column first; it is the easiest to see and the most honest about temptation.

| | Reckless | Responsible | Redemptive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Power:** who holds influence | | | |
| **Person:** how people are regarded | | | |
| **Purpose:** what it builds toward | | | |

Prompts if the room is quiet: Who decides whether a patient is admitted? Who can see a patient's record? What happens to the coordinator's judgment? Who gets credit when it works?

**Output: the team covenant (15 min).** Three to five sentences drawn from the Redemptive column, such as how decisions about people will be made, how patient data will be treated, and what we will refuse to build. Read it aloud at the start of every later session.

**Check-out.** Which of the three enemies is pulling on your heart most in this project, and why?

## Session 1: Lament

The team listens to the people closest to a referral before it names any problem to solve. The outputs are a pain map and a problem statement with no technology in it. This is the session most likely to change the answer, so give it the most time; it can span two meetings, with listening visits between them.

**Opening reading.** Nehemiah 1:3–11. Nehemiah hears the news, sits down and weeps, and prays for days before he plans anything.

**Movement 1: Listen (before and during the session).** Pairs of one builder and one non-builder go and listen. Ask open questions and write down exact words, not interpretations.

| Who to listen to | Where | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A former recuperative care client | Through Illumination, with consent | What was it like to be told you were ready to leave the hospital? Who explained what came next? What did you wait for? |
| Hospital social worker or case manager | On a shift, if the hospital allows | Walk me through your last referral. What did you have to chase? What happens when the answer is no? |
| Referral coordinator | At their desk while referrals arrive (fake or redacted packets only) | What does a hard referral look like? What do you retype? What keeps you from deciding within two hours? |
| Admitting nurse (LVN) | During an admission | What do you wish you had known before the patient arrived? What has intake missed that put someone at risk? |
| Care Coordinator or Housing Case Manager | In a case conference | What do you have to ask the patient again that someone already asked? |

**Movement 2: Remember (40 min).** Build the pain map on a wall: one row per person, sticky notes for what they carry, in their own words. Then name the problem honestly together. Describe the pain, the injustice, the losses and their consequences without softening them. Bring them before God in prayer.

Write the problem statement as *who* is hurting, *when*, and *what it costs them*. If it mentions a fax, a form, an app or AI, rewrite it.

**Movement 3: Repent (25 min).** Each person names one assumption they brought in that the listening challenged, and one way they or their kind of work contributes to what is broken. Starters:

- "I assumed the problem was the paperwork, but…"
- "I assumed speed is what matters most, but…"
- "As a builder, I tend to…"

**Movement 4: Rejoice (15 min).** Name where God is already at work: the staff who answer these referrals every day, the beds that exist at all, the people who did get housed. Give thanks before moving on.

**Parking lot.** Read the solution ideas collected so far, then leave them for Discern.

**Check-out: before moving forward, the team can answer:**

- Whose pain have we heard first-hand, not secondhand?
- Which of our assumptions were challenged?
- What deeper causes may sit beneath the visible problem?
- How might we be contributing to what is broken?
- Are we ready to discern a response, or still rushing?

## Session 2: Discern

The team decides *whether* to build before deciding *what* to build. The output is one named response, the guardrails that go with it, and the conditions that would make the team stop.

**Opening reading.** Romans 12:2. Then reread the problem statement from Lament aloud. Ask two questions side by side: "How might we solve this?" and "How might Jesus respond?"

**Exercise 1: the four responses (60 min).** Take the problem statement and the parking lot. For each response, list real options before judging any of them. Every response must get at least two options, even if they seem unlikely.

| Response | Core question | Options to put on the table for this problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reject | Should a technology product be the solution at all? | More coordinator hours at peak times; a direct phone line between hospital and coordinator; a shorter paper form; changing hospital discharge timing; a standing weekly call with each hospital |
| Receive | Does a redemptive solution already exist? | Intake inside the EHR Illumination already uses (KIPU); the health plan's own authorization portal; a HIPAA-ready form builder; an e-fax service; what other recuperative care programs use |
| Reimagine | Is there something we can redemptively repurpose? | Fixing the fillable PDF so hospitals can type it; adding fields to an existing portal; using an off-the-shelf document reader inside the current process |
| Create | How do we build redemptively? | A purpose-built intake tool, only for the part nothing else covers |

Then mark each option: who it serves, who bears its cost or risk, and whether it lifts the pain named in Lament. Choose one response, or a combination, and write it in one sentence.

**Exercise 2: if the answer includes Create (40 min).** Name the guardrails before any design. Questions to settle:

- **Power.** Which decisions must a person always make? (Admission, denial, anything about a patient's safety.) What may a model do, and what must plain, checkable rules do?
- **Person.** What does the tool do when it is unsure? How does it show its uncertainty so nobody trusts a wrong reading? Who can see patient data, and what must be in place before any real patient's data goes in?
- **Purpose.** Does it serve the patient, or only the throughput? What must it *not* become (for example, a tool that denies people faster)?
- **Misuse.** How could it be exploited, misused or relied on too much? Who is harmed if a field is read wrong with high confidence?
- **Scope.** Where does it stop? What belongs in systems Illumination already runs?

**Exercise 3: stop conditions (15 min).** Write three to five things that would make the team pause or stop, such as: coordinators trust it less than paper; a misread causes a wrong admission; the cost of keeping it running falls on people who did not choose it; real data would go in before the compliance work is done.

**Who must be in this discernment.** Not the builders alone. The coordinator, the nurse and, if possible, the person with lived experience should agree with the response.

**Check-out: before moving forward, the team can answer:**

- Have we clearly named the response we believe is faithful?
- What assumptions or desires shaped the decision?
- Who was included, and who was missing?
- What risks, misuse or unintended consequences can we already see?
- What would cause us to reconsider or stop?

## Session 3: Build

The team turns the discerned response into a plan of small slices, each one followed by a pause. If Discern chose Reject or Receive, this session plans that change instead of software, using the same rhythm. The outputs are the sliced plan and a trade-off log.

**Opening reading.** Exodus 33:15 and Proverbs 16:9. Then pray for the people on the pain map by name or by role.

**The rhythm for every slice: pray, prototype, pause.**

1. **Pray.** Before each slice, pray and reread the covenant and the problem statement. The playbook's shorthand for AI-assisted work is "pray before you prompt." It fits a team that writes code with an AI assistant.
2. **Prototype.** Build the smallest thing that a person on the pain map can try, with fake data. Show it to them, not just to each other.
3. **Pause.** Ask the pause questions below. Give thanks for what was made and who made it.

**Exercise 1: order the slices from the pain map (45 min).** Put each pain on a card. Rank them by how much each one hurts the patient, not by how easy it is to build. The first slice addresses the top card. Write each slice as "*role* can *do what* so that *pain is lifted*."

| Order | Slice (role can do what, so that) | Pain it lifts (from Lament) | Who tries it | Pause date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | | | | |
| 2 | | | | |
| 3 | | | | |
| 4 | | | | |

**Exercise 2: the pause questions (15 min to agree).** Asked at the end of every slice:

- Is this still what we discerned, or has it drifted?
- Are the people involved, builders included, being loved well? Is anyone exhausted?
- Did the person who tried it feel more trusted or less?
- Is there a feature we should remove, delay or not build?
- Does this increase love of God and neighbor? Does it tell the truth? Does it strengthen real relationships rather than replace them?

**Exercise 3: the trade-off log (20 min).** Start a running list of choices where speed, cost, scale or convenience pulled against faithfulness. Write down what was given up and why. Likely early entries:

| Date | Trade-off | What we chose | What we gave up | Revisit when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | Real vs. fake patient data while building | | | |
| | A cheaper model vs. a more accurate reading | | | |
| | Building fast at a hackathon pace vs. rest for the team | | | |
| | Adding a role's screen vs. leaving it to a system that already exists | | | |

**Team care.** Agree on working hours, a day of rest, and that anyone may call a pause. A product is not redemptive if the process exhausts, silences or manipulates the people making it.

**Check-out: before moving forward, the team can answer:**

- Where have we intentionally invited the Spirit into the work?
- Are we holding the roadmap with open hands?
- What trade-offs are we making between speed, profit, scale and faithfulness?
- Where might self-reliance, control or ambition be shaping the work?
- Are we willing to pause, change direction or stop?

## Session 4: Steward

The team decides who will care for what it makes, how it will judge the fruit, and what must be true before real patients' information goes in. The outputs are a stewardship plan and a release gate. Hold this session before anything is released, not after.

**Opening reading.** Genesis 2:15 and the parable of the talents, Matthew 25:14–30.

**Movement 1: Tend (30 min).** Name who will look after the tool once the builders move on.

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who owns it day to day at Illumination? | |
| Who fixes it when it breaks, and how fast? | |
| Who pays for hosting and AI use, and from which budget? | |
| How do coordinators and referrers report a problem? | |
| When would we choose to retire it, and what would we hand back? | |

**The release gate (20 min).** List what must be true before any real patient's information goes in. Write it as a checklist and keep it in the project. Prompts for this domain:

- [ ] Signed business associate agreements with every service that touches patient data
- [ ] A record of who viewed or changed each referral
- [ ] Sign-in that can't be claimed by someone else
- [ ] Tested on real handwriting, de-identified, with the error rate known
- [ ] Coordinators trained, and the paper process still available as a fallback
- [ ] Test accounts and fake records removed

**Movement 2: Examine (40 min).** Agree how the fruit will be judged across people, product and industry, and when. Choose measures for each, including at least one that could show harm.

| Area | What to look at | How and when we'll check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People | Do patients wait less in a hospital bed? Do coordinators feel more trusted or more watched? Is anyone's judgment being replaced? | |
| Product | Is it doing what it was made to do? How often is a reading wrong with high confidence? | |
| Industry | Does it change how hospitals refer, for better or worse? Does it push hard cases away? | |

What comes back from Examine leads back to Lament: a new pain or a harm we didn't foresee starts the cycle again.

**Movement 3: Testify (20 min).** Plan how the story will be told at a demo, to Illumination's board or at a FaithTech gathering. It names whom the product serves, what changed, the risks that remain, the responsibility the team is taking, and what the process did in each builder's life. Credit the staff and the people who were listened to. Make no one the hero but God.

**Check-out: before moving forward, the team can answer:**

- Should this be released, or go on to the next stage?
- Who is responsible for tending it over time?
- What fruit are we seeing in people, the product and the industry?
- How will we tell the story truthfully and point praise toward God?

## Session 5: Open the envelope

Only now does the team look at the app that already exists, and judge it against what the workshop produced, not the other way round. The output is a decision to keep it, change it or start over, with the reasons written down.

**Opening reading.** Psalm 127:1. Then read the covenant and the problem statement one more time.

**Exercise 1: the walk-through (30 min).** Someone who did not build it walks through the existing app as each person on the pain map would use it. Everyone else takes notes against the workshop outputs, not against their own taste.

**Exercise 2: compare (45 min).** Fill one row per workshop output.

| Workshop output | What the workshop concluded | What the existing app does | Fits, partly fits, or conflicts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Covenant (power, person, purpose) | | | |
| Problem statement (Lament) | | | |
| Top pains on the pain map | | | |
| Named response (Discern) | | | |
| Guardrails | | | |
| Stop conditions | | | |
| Slice order (Build) | | | |
| Trade-off log | | | |
| Stewardship plan and release gate | | | |

Also list what the app does that the workshop never asked for. Each one is a question for the team: did it come from listening, or from what was easy or impressive to build?

**Exercise 3: decide (30 min).** Use the comparison to choose one path.

| If the workshop found | Then |
| --- | --- |
| Discern chose Reject or Receive | Retire or set aside the app; carry out the response instead. Keep what was learned. |
| A different core problem, or a guardrail the app breaks at its foundation | Start over, reusing only the parts that clearly serve the new problem. |
| The same core problem, but different priorities, extra scope, or gaps in guardrails or stewardship | Change it: cut what wasn't asked for, reorder the backlog by the pain map, and close the gaps before release. |
| The app matches the outputs | Keep it, and take it through the Steward release gate. |

Whichever path is chosen, give thanks for the work already done and for the people who did it.

**The cycle continues.** Book the next Lament session: listen again to the same people after they have tried what was decided. Stewardship leads back to lament.

## Facilitator prep

Do these before Session 0. The listening visits take the longest to arrange, so start there.

- [ ] Confirm the sessions and dates with the team and with Illumination
- [ ] Ask Illumination whether a former recuperative care client can take part, with consent and a stipend
- [ ] Arrange listening visits: a coordinator's desk, an admission, and a hospital social worker willing to talk
- [ ] Prepare fake or fully de-identified referral packets for any exercise that shows paperwork
- [ ] Seal the envelope: put the existing app, its requirements and screenshots out of sight, and brief anyone who has seen them
- [ ] Copy the playbook pages for each session, and the "before moving forward" questions as check-out cards
- [ ] Choose the prayer and Scripture lead for each session
- [ ] Set up a wall or shared board for the pain map, the parking lot and the postures grid
- [ ] Pick someone to type each session's outputs into this doc afterwards
- [ ] Choose a place to keep the covenant, problem statement, trade-off log and release gate between sessions
